HW5 Step 3 · Structure from Motion (Planar Object, 4 Viewpoints)

Recovers each camera's pose from 4 photos of one flat object, then triangulates extra boundary points across the recovered cameras to reconstruct the object's outline.

1. Camera model

A pinhole camera maps a 3D world point \(X_w=(X,Y,Z)\) to a homogeneous image point via

$$s\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\1\end{bmatrix} = K[R\,|\,t]\begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\\Z\\1\end{bmatrix} = P\,X_w$$

\(K\) (3x3) holds the intrinsics \((f_x,f_y,c_x,c_y)\); \([R|t]\) is the extrinsic pose (world→camera rotation + translation); \(P=K[R|t]\) is that camera's 3x4 projection matrix.

2. Recovering the "motion": each camera's pose from the 4 known corners

Because the object is planar, the world frame is placed on the object itself: origin at the top-left corner, \(X\) along the top edge, \(Y\) down the side, \(Z=0\) on the surface. With the measured width \(W\) and height \(H\), the corners have known world coordinates \((0,0,0),\ (W,0,0),\ (W,H,0),\ (0,H,0)\).

2a. Homography. With \(Z=0\) the third column of \(R\) drops out:

$$s\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\1\end{bmatrix} = K\,[\,r_1\ r_2\ r_3\ t\,]\begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\\0\\1\end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{K\,[\,r_1\ r_2\ t\,]}_{H}\begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\\1\end{bmatrix}$$

\(H\) is a 3×3 homography. Eliminating \(s\) (for \(h = \text{vec}(H)\)) gives two linear equations per corner:

$$\begin{bmatrix}-X & -Y & -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 & xX & xY & x\\ 0 & 0 & 0 & -X & -Y & -1 & yX & yY & y\end{bmatrix} h = 0$$

4 corners give \(A\in\mathbb R^{8\times9}\), and \(h\) is the right singular vector of \(A\) with the smallest singular value (DLT).

2b. Pose from \(H\). \(B = K^{-1}H = \lambda[\,r_1\ r_2\ t\,]\). Since \(\|r_1\|=1\): \(\lambda = 1/\|b_1\|\), \(r_1 = \lambda b_1,\ r_2 = \lambda b_2,\ r_3 = r_1\times r_2,\ t = \lambda b_3\). The sign is chosen so that \(t_z>0\) (the object is in front of the camera). Noise makes \([r_1 r_2 r_3]\) slightly non-orthogonal, so it is projected to the nearest rotation \(R = UV^T\) (from its SVD).

2c. Refinement. cv2.solvePnP starts from such an estimate and minimises the reprojection error. The result shows both, and how far apart they are. The camera's position in the world is \(C = -R^Tt\). Doing this once per photo gives 4 camera poses, which is the "motion".

3. Recovering the "structure": triangulating the rest of the boundary

Any other point on the boundary is not assumed known - its 3D position is recovered from where it lands in the (now-known) cameras. For view \(i\) with \(P_i\), a 2D observation \((x_i,y_i)\) of the same 3D point \(X\) satisfies \(x_i \times (P_iX) = 0\), linear in \(X\), giving 2 rows per view:

$$x_i\,P_i^{(3)} - P_i^{(1)} = 0 \qquad y_i\,P_i^{(3)} - P_i^{(2)} = 0$$

(\(P^{(k)}\) = row \(k\) of \(P\)). Stacking these rows from all 4 views gives \(AX=0\); the least-squares solution is the right singular vector of \(A\) with the smallest singular value (linear/DLT triangulation). Doing this for every clicked boundary point recovers the object's 3D boundary.

4. Built-in validation

Two numbers below check the reconstruction rather than just trusting it: (a) the 4 corners are re-triangulated as if unknown and compared back to the known rectangle - the corner recovery error; (b) a plane is fit (via SVD) to every reconstructed point and its RMS distance is the planarity residual - small for both confirms the pipeline, since the true object is flat and its corners are known.

5. Camera intrinsics \(K\)

In order of preference: (1) your HW2 chessboard calibration, if these photos came from the same camera; (2) the photo's EXIF 35 mm-equivalent focal length \(f_{35}\), using \(f_{px} = f_{35}/43.27\cdot\sqrt{w^2+h^2}\) (43.27 mm is the diagonal of a 35 mm frame) with the principal point at the image centre; (3) an assumed 60° horizontal field of view. The results report which one was used.
