HW5 Step 3 · Structure from Motion (Planar Object, 4 Viewpoints)

Recovers each camera's pose from 4 photos of one flat object, then triangulates extra boundary points across the recovered cameras to reconstruct the object's outline.

1. Camera model

A pinhole camera maps a 3D world point \(X_w=(X,Y,Z)\) to a homogeneous image point via

$$s\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\1\end{bmatrix} = K[R\,|\,t]\begin{bmatrix}X\\Y\\Z\\1\end{bmatrix} = P\,X_w$$

\(K\) (3x3) holds the intrinsics \((f_x,f_y,c_x,c_y)\); \([R|t]\) is the extrinsic pose (world→camera rotation + translation); \(P=K[R|t]\) is that camera's 3x4 projection matrix.

2. Recovering the "motion": pose per viewpoint via PnP

Because the object is planar, the world frame is defined to sit on the object's own plane (\(Z=0\)) with its known real-world size on that plane. A rectangle of width \(W\), height \(H\) then has known world coordinates for its 4 corners:

$$(0,0,0),\ (W,0,0),\ (W,H,0),\ (0,H,0)$$

Clicking those same 4 corners in a photo gives 4 world↔image correspondences - exactly what Perspective-n-Point (PnP) needs to solve for that photo's \((R,t)\) given \(K\):

$$\text{solvePnP}(\{X_{w,\text{corner}}\}, \{x_{\text{pixel}}\}, K)\ \rightarrow\ R,\ t$$

Repeating this once per photo gives 4 independent camera poses - the "motion" side of structure-from-motion.

3. Recovering the "structure": triangulating the rest of the boundary

Any other point on the boundary is not assumed known - its 3D position is recovered from where it lands in the (now-known) cameras. For view \(i\) with \(P_i\), a 2D observation \((x_i,y_i)\) of the same 3D point \(X\) satisfies \(x_i \times (P_iX) = 0\), linear in \(X\), giving 2 rows per view:

$$x_i\,P_i^{(3)} - P_i^{(1)} = 0 \qquad y_i\,P_i^{(3)} - P_i^{(2)} = 0$$

(\(P^{(k)}\) = row \(k\) of \(P\)). Stacking these rows from all 4 views gives \(AX=0\); the least-squares solution is the right singular vector of \(A\) with the smallest singular value (linear/DLT triangulation). Doing this for every clicked boundary point recovers the object's 3D boundary.

4. Built-in validation

Two numbers below check the reconstruction rather than just trusting it: (a) the 4 corners are re-triangulated as if unknown and compared back to the known rectangle - the corner recovery error; (b) a plane is fit (via SVD) to every reconstructed point and its RMS distance is the planarity residual - small for both confirms the pipeline, since the true object is flat and its corners are known.
