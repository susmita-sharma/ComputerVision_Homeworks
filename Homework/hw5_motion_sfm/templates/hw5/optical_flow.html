HW5 Step 1 · Optical Flow 30+ sec clip

Upload a video with at least ~30 seconds of real motion in it (something moving through the scene, or the camera itself panning/walking). Dense optical flow (Farneback) is computed between every consecutive frame pair and color-coded into a second video.

What optical flow actually measures

Optical flow \(\mathbf{v}(x,y) = (u,v)\) is the apparent 2D velocity of image brightness at each pixel between two frames, under the brightness-constancy assumption \(I(x,y,t) = I(x+u,y+v,t+1)\). It is not true 3D motion - it's a projection of it, and it is undefined (zero) anywhere the image has no local texture (the flat interior of a wall gives no information about how it moved, even if it did).

See the Tracking page for the full derivation from the brightness-constancy equation.

  • When motion happened: the left plot's "% pixels moving" curve marks the timestamps where something in the scene was actually displacing between frames >1px, vs. moments the scene was static.
  • How fast: mean flow magnitude (px/frame) tracks relative speed over time - peaks correspond to the fastest-moving moments in the clip (peak here: {{ "%.2f"|format(result.overall.peak_mean_mag) }} px/frame).
  • Which direction: the direction histogram (right plot) shows whether motion was dominantly one way (a tight peak - e.g. consistent camera pan or an object crossing frame) or scattered (multiple/complex motions, or mostly noise).
  • How much of the frame moved: the moving-area fraction distinguishes a small object moving through an otherwise static scene (low %) from broad motion like a camera pan (high %, often >50%).
