HW5 · Optical Flow, Tracking & Structure from Motion

The full workflow, in order. Each step's page explains its maths, runs on your own videos/photos, and has a Download all outputs (ZIP) button so you can keep the sample output of every module for the submission.

0 · Capture

  • Two videos, each with ≥ 30 s containing motion. Good choices: people walking past a fixed phone, traffic, a pet, a ball being thrown, or one video where you pan/walk with the camera (so the flow shows camera ego-motion as well as object motion). Hold the phone steady, or use a tripod for the object-motion video.
  • Four photos of one flat rectangular object (book, box face, picture frame) from 4 clearly different viewpoints. Same phone, same zoom, same orientation. Measure the object's width/height with a ruler and note where each photo was taken from.

1 · Optical flow + tracking video (per video)

Upload Video 1 and Video 2 (separate slots). For each, the app computes dense optical flow on every consecutive frame pair and renders a real-time 2×2 video: moving objects boxed with IDs, speed and direction; corner features with motion trails; the color-coded flow field; flow vectors; and the camera-compensated motion map that decides what counts as an object. You watch frames appear live while it processes.

Saves: flow_tracking.webm, stats.png, stats.csv, objects.csv, summary.md (the "what can be inferred" write-up with numbers), key frames, and the frame pair for step 2.

2 · Tracking equations, bilinear interpolation, validation (per video)

The page derives Lucas-Kanade from brightness constancy and sets up the two-frame tracking problem. It also derives bilinear interpolation. Then, for each video's consecutive frame pair (one click from step 1), it tracks points with the from-scratch implementation. The predicted locations are compared with the actual pixel locations measured independently by template matching, and with OpenCV. A worked example shows the real numbers (normal matrix, Newton iterations, the bilinear lookup).

Saves: annotated frame, zoom crops, tracking_table.csv, tracking_report.md, both frames.

3 · Structure from motion (4 viewpoints, planar object)

Upload the 4 photos and click the 4 corners plus a few extra points in each. The app computes each camera's pose (homography → \(R, t\), refined with PnP), triangulates the extra points from all 4 views, and estimates the object's boundary, area and edge lengths. It reports the camera parameters \(K\) and every camera position, and shows the full matrix workout for each view.

Saves: the 4 input photos, annotated photos with re-projection, 3D and top-down plots, cameras.csv, points.json, sfm_report.md (all calculations).
