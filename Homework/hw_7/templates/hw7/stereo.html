HW7 Part 1 · Calibrated Stereo Object Measurement

Sizes come from 3D points recovered with the camera calibration and the measured baseline, not from a pixel-to-mm ratio.

Stereo geometry

After rectification, a 3D point appears on the same row of both images, shifted by the disparity \(d\):

$$d = x_L - x_R - (c_{x,L} - c_{x,R}), \qquad Z = \frac{fB}{d}$$

$$X = \frac{(x-c_x)Z}{f}, \qquad Y = \frac{(y-c_y)Z}{f}$$

The essential matrix gives only the direction of the camera movement. The measured baseline \(B\) sets its length, which is what makes the result metric. A length is then \(\|P_i - P_j\|\) between two 3D points. A circle is fitted to its rim points in their best 3D plane, so a tilted circle still gives its true diameter.

Step B · Select the object points

Choose a shape, then click on the rectified left image. Each point is matched along its row in the right image. If the match marker is wrong, click the correct place on the right image. Its row stays locked.

  • Rectangle: 4 corners in order around the shape.
  • Circle: 3 or more points spread around the rim. 6 or more is better.
  • Polygon: every vertex in order, then Finish shape.
