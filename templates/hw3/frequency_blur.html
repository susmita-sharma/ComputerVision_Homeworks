HW3 · Blur: Spatial vs. Frequency

Upload an image, pick a blur kernel, and the app runs the same blur two independent ways: (1) direct spatial convolution, sliding the kernel over the image, and (2) multiplying the image's spectrum by the kernel's spectrum in the frequency domain and inverse-transforming. The two outputs should be numerically identical up to floating-point rounding - which is exactly the convolution theorem.

Theory: convolution in space ≡ multiplication in frequency

Let f(x, y) be the image and h(x, y) the blur kernel. 2D linear convolution is defined as

\( (f * h)(x,y) \;=\; \sum_{m}\sum_{n} f(m,n)\,h(x-m,\,y-n) \)

The 2D Discrete Fourier Transform of a size-M×N signal is

\( F(u,v) \;=\; \sum_{x=0}^{M-1}\sum_{y=0}^{N-1} f(x,y)\, e^{-j 2\pi (ux/M + vy/N)} \)

Derivation. Take the DFT of the convolution directly:

\( \mathcal{F}\{f*h\}(u,v) = \sum_{x,y} \Big[\sum_{m,n} f(m,n)h(x-m,y-n)\Big] e^{-j2\pi(ux/M+vy/N)} \)

Swap the order of summation and substitute x' = x - m, y' = y - n:

\( = \sum_{m,n} f(m,n) \sum_{x',y'} h(x',y')\, e^{-j2\pi(u(x'+m)/M + v(y'+n)/N)} \)

The inner sum is exactly H(u,v) times a phase factor that only depends on (m,n):

\( = \Big[\sum_{m,n} f(m,n)\, e^{-j2\pi(um/M+vn/N)}\Big] \cdot H(u,v) \;=\; F(u,v)\,H(u,v) \)

Result (convolution theorem):

\( f * h \;\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\; F(u,v)\cdot H(u,v) \)

Two practical details this implementation takes care of, so the spatial and frequency results actually line up pixel-for-pixel:

  1. Circular vs. linear convolution. Multiplying two DFTs of the same size M×N gives their circular convolution, which wraps around at the image borders. To get ordinary (linear) convolution, both the image and the kernel are zero-padded to size (M+k_h-1)×(N+k_w-1) before transforming - the classic "linear convolution via FFT" trick - and the result is cropped back using the kernel's anchor offset.
  2. Boundary handling. The spatial-domain path uses zero-padded borders (cv2.BORDER_CONSTANT) so it matches the implicit zero-padding used on the frequency side.
